## Executive Summary

EEC already has a strong base: an AI Tutor, teacher controls, assessments, progress tracking, parent communication, wellbeing records, attendance monitoring and intervention tools. However, the selected research papers ask for more than the presence of a feature. They also expect the feature to be connected to a complete school process and to produce a measurable educational benefit.

**Estimated overall completion: 45% — Partly done**

This means EEC has many of the building blocks, but the full research-backed system is not finished yet.

- **Mostly done:** 5 research areas
- **Partly done:** 11 research areas
- **Early stage or upcoming:** 9 research areas
- **Fully done and scientifically proven:** 0 research areas

The strongest areas are teacher support, parent–school communication, step-by-step learning support and student choice. The biggest remaining work is proving learning impact, connecting student information into one safe support process, adding dedicated safety workflows and measuring fairness and wellbeing outcomes.

## EEC Features That Already Exist

Before looking at each paper, these are the main product strengths already available in EEC:

1. **Different portals for different users:** Students, teachers, parents and school leaders have their own access and views.
2. **AI Tutor based on school material:** The tutor can use learning material approved and uploaded by teachers. It can explain topics, give examples, create quizzes, help with homework, prepare notes, make flashcards and show mind maps.
3. **Teacher control:** Teachers can prepare lessons, create different difficulty levels, produce assessments and review learning paths before students receive them.
4. **Assessment and progress tracking:** EEC stores exams, practice attempts, starting-level assessments, reading results, writing results and subject progress.
5. **Parent–school communication:** Parents and teachers can use chat, meetings, observations, complaints, excuse letters and notifications.
6. **Student wellbeing information:** The school can record mood, stress, social participation, health concerns and teacher or parent observations.
7. **Language-learning support:** EEC supports oral reading, pronunciation, fluency, grammar, vocabulary and writing feedback over time.
8. **Early warnings and teacher action:** Teachers can see attendance or performance concerns, identify weak topics and record support given to a student.
9. **Basic quality and security checks:** EEC has automated checks for product behavior, data protection and whether AI answers use the correct school material.

## Detailed Research Comparison

### 1. Alhassan — Effective Teaching Practices and Inclusion of Students with Special Needs

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Alhassan  
**EEC completion:** **55% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Giving every child the same lesson is not true inclusion. Teachers need ways to change the difficulty, pace, teaching style and support for each child.

**What EEC already has:**

- The AI Tutor can explain the same topic in several ways, including simple explanations, examples, visual explanations, quizzes, notes, flashcards and mind maps.
- Teachers can prepare Foundation, Standard and Extension versions of learning material.
- Teachers can create and approve a different learning path for an individual student.
- Student records can hold learning preferences and limited information about learning difficulties.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs a safe and complete special-needs support profile. It should help teachers adjust pace, reading level, presentation style and support without labelling or unfairly judging the child.

### 6. Bell & Fandrem — Parental Experiences with Cooperation to Overcome Cyberbullying

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Bell & Fandrem  
**EEC completion:** **70% — Mostly done**

**What the paper means:** Parents and schools must work together when a child faces cyberbullying.

**What EEC already has:**

- Parents and teachers have direct chat.
- Parent–teacher meetings can be arranged in person, by phone or by video.
- Parents and teachers can submit concerns and observations about a student.
- Complaints, excuse letters and notifications provide formal communication routes.
- School staff can record how serious a concern is and whether follow-up is required.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs a dedicated cyberbullying and child-safety reporting process, including confidential reporting, urgent escalation, assigned responsibility, action history and case closure.

### 7. Bruner & Kucirkova — The Price of Learning

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Bruner & Kucirkova  
**EEC completion:** **45% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** A product is not educationally good simply because it is expensive or has many features. It must create real learning value.

**What EEC already has:**

- The product is regularly checked for errors, security problems and incorrect AI behavior.
- EEC checks whether the AI is using the school's learning material instead of inventing unrelated answers.
- The project keeps a list of completed features and known gaps.
- EEC already has several features connected to learning, such as practice, feedback, reading and writing assessment.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC must measure whether students actually learn better, remember longer and need less teacher correction after using each major feature. More features should not be treated as proof of better education.

### 10. Colombo et al. — Cognitive Reserve, Depression and Sleep

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Colombo et al.  
**EEC completion:** **50% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** A person's performance can be affected by wider life conditions such as sleep and emotional health. Marks alone do not tell the whole story.

**What EEC already has:**

- School staff can record mood, stress, energy, attention, confidence and social participation.
- Teachers can record sleep quality and behavior changes in student observations.
- Parents can share observations about sleep, anxiety, physical activity and emotional condition.
- Attendance and marks give teachers more context when reviewing a student.

**What is still upcoming:** These details are stored in different places and are not yet joined into one careful support view. Sensitive wellbeing information should guide a human review, not allow the AI to diagnose a student.

### 12. Eriksen, Tharaldsen & Bru — Social-Emotional Learning and Academic Engagement

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Eriksen, Tharaldsen & Bru  
**EEC completion:** **45% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** A student may understand the lesson but feel bored, worried or disconnected. That is different from not understanding the topic.

**What EEC already has:**

- EEC keeps academic progress and participation information separately.
- Participation can include lesson-material use, time spent and quiz activity.
- Teachers can record attention, confidence, mood, stress and classroom involvement.
- The AI Tutor has a mode that tries to make a topic more interesting when a student loses interest.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC cannot yet reliably tell the difference between “does not understand” and “understands but is not engaged.” Teacher feedback and student self-report need to be part of that decision.

### 17. Jacobsen et al. — Science Education for Toddlers

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Jacobsen et al.  
**EEC completion:** **30% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** Children also learn through nature, observation, experiments and real-world exploration—not only through a screen.

**What EEC already has:**

- The AI Tutor can connect a lesson to real-life examples.
- Teachers can add experiments, materials, worksheets and practical activities to a lesson.
- Schools can upload experiment instructions and activity resources.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs a proper “take learning offline” feature with simple activity steps, required materials, adult supervision, safety instructions and a short reflection after the activity.

### 18. Keles et al. — Validity of Early-Grade Screening Tools

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Keles et al.  
**EEC completion:** **60% — Mostly done**

**What the paper means:** A short starting assessment can identify a child who may need help, but it should lead to levels of support and should never act as a final diagnosis.

**What EEC already has:**

- Teachers can create, review and publish starting-level assessments.
- Student answers are marked and stored securely.
- A low result can create an early progress record and alert the teacher.
- Teachers can create a support plan, record actions and follow the status and outcome.
- Teachers decide whether an AI-prepared learning path should be given to the student.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs clearly defined support levels, approved screening questions, confidence checks and follow-up assessments. The AI should recommend a review, not diagnose a learning disability.

### 21. Kucirkova — Academia–Industry Partnerships in EdTech

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Kucirkova  
**EEC completion:** **45% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Education products should be improved through a repeated cycle: research, build, test with schools, measure the result and improve again.

**What EEC already has:**

- Research summaries and product-gap reviews are kept with the project.
- The team tracks completed and missing AI Tutor capabilities.
- Product behavior and AI quality can be checked repeatedly.
- Teachers and students have places to provide feedback.

**What is still upcoming:** Each important feature needs a clear educational goal, a measure of success, teacher feedback, student outcome results and a record of what was changed because of the findings.

### 22. Kucirkova et al. — Evidence Indicators for K12 EdTech

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Kucirkova et al.  
**EEC completion:** **30% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** An education product should be judged in five areas: Does it improve learning? Does it work in real schools? Is it ethical? Is it fair to different groups? Does it suit the school and its environment?

**What EEC already has:**

- User access controls, privacy protections and activity records support safe and responsible use.
- Teachers control important learning material and student learning paths.
- Different portals support real school roles such as student, teacher, parent and administrator.
- EEC stores useful information about learning, wellbeing, attendance and communication.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC does not yet have a working five-area scorecard. It needs real learning studies, teacher-workload measurement, fairness checks between student groups and checks for device access and screen-time impact.

### 24. Lerang et al. — Instructional Support and Student Engagement

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Lerang et al.  
**EEC completion:** **70% — Mostly done**

**What the paper means:** Good teaching support moves a student through a helpful sequence: hint, example, smaller step, question, feedback and reflection.

**What EEC already has:**

- Homework Help guides the student with questions instead of immediately giving the answer.
- Students can request a simpler explanation, an example or a real-world connection.
- Quizzes and quick-check questions provide immediate feedback.
- Teachers can prepare three difficulty levels.
- EEC can explain why an answer is wrong and provide notes, flashcards and mind maps.
- Students have a journal for reflection.

**What is still upcoming:** These features need to work as one continuous journey. EEC should remember which hint was used, allow a retry, check understanding again and show the teacher whether the support worked.

### 27. Pérez-Marco et al. — Patterns of School Absenteeism

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Pérez-Marco et al.  
**EEC completion:** **55% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Absence can be connected to the classroom environment, difficult schoolwork, low engagement or personal concerns. It should not be treated only as a number.

**What EEC already has:**

- Attendance is recorded by date and can also be viewed by subject.
- Teachers and parents can view attendance reports.
- Low attendance can create a notification.
- EEC can combine attendance with recent marks and whether performance is improving or falling.
- Teachers can record an action taken for a student who may be at risk.

**What is still upcoming:** Attendance, engagement, classroom experience, wellbeing and the student's own explanation need to appear together before the school decides what support is needed.

### 28. Ranginkaman et al. — Measuring Children's Optimism

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Ranginkaman et al.  
**EEC completion:** **15% — Upcoming**

**What the paper means:** A child's expectations about the future may be useful to understand over time, but they should be measured carefully.

**What EEC already has:** EEC can store progress over time, wellbeing information and teacher or parent observations. This provides a place for future research information if the school decides it is appropriate.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC has no approved optimism questionnaire or dedicated optimism feature. It should not guess a child's optimism from normal AI Tutor conversations.

### 29. Sagen & Ten Braak — Growth Mindset Measurement

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Sagen & Ten Braak  
**EEC completion:** **20% — Upcoming**

**What the paper means:** A child's belief that ability can improve should not be judged from one or two answers.

**What EEC already has:**

- Students can retry practice and review weaker topics.
- EEC stores repeated attempts and progress over time.
- The tutor uses encouraging language and treats mistakes as part of learning.
- Reflection journals and teacher observations can show a broader picture.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC does not have an approved growth-mindset assessment. It should support positive learning behavior without giving the child a permanent “mindset score.”

### 30. Smedsrud — Supporting Play

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Smedsrud  
**EEC completion:** **30% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** Play and the natural environment are important parts of learning. Personalization should sometimes send the child away from the device.

**What EEC already has:**

- EEC includes learning games and interactive activities.
- Teachers can prepare experiments, worksheets and practical work.
- Lessons can contain teacher-led and student-led activity stages.
- The AI Tutor can connect topics to everyday situations.

**What is still upcoming:** Most existing games are still screen-based. EEC needs off-screen play suggestions, simple safety guidance and a way for the student or teacher to record what was learned.

### 31. Street et al. — Classroom Management

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Street et al.  
**EEC completion:** **35% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** New teachers often need practical help to handle difficult classroom situations.

**What EEC already has:**

- Teachers can use AI to prepare lessons, different difficulty levels and quick understanding checks.
- Teachers can record behavior, attention, stress and communication concerns.
- EEC can flag students needing academic support and help the teacher plan an action.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs a dedicated classroom-management coach with realistic scenarios, safe response choices, explanations and follow-up guidance. It should support teacher judgement, not automatically discipline a student.

### 32. Strohmeier et al. — Social Relatedness and Immigrant Adolescents

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Strohmeier et al.  
**EEC completion:** **50% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Learning a language also depends on belonging, friendship and feeling safe to communicate—not only grammar and vocabulary.

**What EEC already has:**

- Reading practice checks pronunciation, fluency, confidence, speed and difficult words.
- Writing practice checks grammar, vocabulary, clarity, structure and creativity.
- Teachers can publish reading material and writing prompts.
- EEC keeps a student's language progress over time.
- Chat and student observations provide some information about communication and social interaction.

**What is still upcoming:** Language learning needs group activities, peer communication, belonging feedback and culturally suitable material. These social factors are not yet connected to the language-learning plan.

### 35. Tvedt et al. — Teacher Support and Engagement

**Authors shown in the DOCX:** Tvedt et al.  
**EEC completion:** **75% — Mostly done**

**What the paper means:** Technology should strengthen the teacher's support for the student, not try to replace the teacher.

**What EEC already has:**

- Teachers choose the school material used by the AI Tutor.
- Teachers review and publish individual learning paths.
- Teachers can prepare lessons, assignments, questions, reading material and writing prompts.
- Dashboards show low progress, attendance concerns and students who may need support.
- Teachers can record the action taken and later record the result.
- Parent meetings, observations and chat keep people involved.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs one simple teacher screen that brings together the evidence, the AI suggestion, teacher approval or changes, the action taken and the student's later progress.

### 46. Situational Student Engagement

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **40% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Engagement can change from one lesson to the next. It depends on the activity, difficulty, teacher interaction and situation; it is not a fixed quality of the student.

**What EEC already has:**

- EEC can review recent material use, time spent and quiz participation.
- Participation is kept separate from academic progress.
- Teachers can record attention, mood, confidence, stress and classroom engagement.
- The AI Tutor offers different activity styles and a re-engagement option.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC currently shows general participation, not true moment-by-moment engagement. It needs short student feedback and teacher context for each activity before it can explain why engagement changed.

### 55. Equity and Intersectionality

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **50% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Student success should not be defined only by examination marks. Health, confidence, relationships, participation and personal circumstances also matter.

**What EEC already has:**

- EEC records mood, stress, social participation and behavior changes.
- Teacher and parent observations can include health, emotion, confidence and communication.
- Achievements can include academics, sport, extracurricular work and other areas.
- Attendance, wellbeing, support history and parent communication provide a wider student picture.

**What is still upcoming:** Academic marks still have a large influence on progress and risk decisions. EEC needs fairness checks to make sure recommendations do not disadvantage students because of language, disability, gender, background or access to technology.

### 57. Applying Science of Learning to EdTech — EVER

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **35% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** Every important education feature should state why it should help learning, what result is expected and how that result will be checked.

**What EEC already has:**

- Quizzes and flashcards support practice and recall.
- Review reminders support learning over time.
- Homework Help uses guided questions.
- Starting assessments and teacher support records form part of a student-help process.
- EEC can check whether AI answers are based on school material.

**What is still upcoming:** Each major feature needs its own plain statement of purpose, expected learning benefit, measurement method, school trial result and improvement history. Technical testing alone does not prove learning impact.

### 65. Beyond Personalization — Democratic Learning with AI

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **65% — Mostly done**

**What the paper means:** Personalized learning should still give the student meaningful choices. The AI should not control every learning decision.

**What EEC already has:**

- Students can choose their subject and topic.
- Students can choose explanations, examples, quizzes, visual help, notes, mind maps, flashcards, homework help, real-world connections and practice level.
- Students can ask their own curriculum questions.
- Advanced practice includes “why,” “how,” analysis and scenario questions.
- Teachers approve learning paths before students receive them.

**What is still upcoming:** Students need long-term goals and clear controls to accept, reject, postpone or request another recommendation. The product should also explain why a recommendation was made.

### 78. Early Mathematics Skills and Executive Function

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **30% — Early stage / upcoming**

**What the paper means:** Mathematics, reading, memory, attention and planning can affect one another. A child should not always be viewed as separate subject scores.

**What EEC already has:**

- Teacher and parent reports show progress across subjects.
- Student dashboards bring together subjects, courses, progress, attendance and achievements.
- Reading, writing, exams and practice results stay connected to the same student.
- Wellbeing and teacher observations add non-academic context.

**What is still upcoming:** The separate records are not yet joined into a safe cross-subject understanding. EEC also has no direct and approved way to assess planning, working memory or other executive skills.

### 80. Children's Critical Thinking

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **50% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Children should practise thinking, explaining and making decisions from an early age—not only memorising answers.

**What EEC already has:**

- Advanced practice includes analysis, inference, “why,” “how” and real-life scenarios.
- Teachers can create extension questions that require deeper thinking.
- Quick understanding questions help expose misunderstandings.
- Homework Help asks guiding questions instead of giving the complete answer immediately.
- Interactive activities provide some problem-solving practice.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC needs age-appropriate critical-thinking levels, clear teacher guidance and a way to measure progress from simple observation to evidence-based reasoning.

### 81. Academic Stress

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **45% — Partly done**

**What the paper means:** Constant pressure to achieve can harm a student's emotional health. The system should not always push for more work or a higher score.

**What EEC already has:**

- School staff can record academic stress, mood, confidence, attention and social participation.
- Teacher observations can highlight stress or behavior changes.
- AI feedback is designed to be encouraging and not shame the student.
- Human follow-up and parent notification can be recorded when needed.

**What is still upcoming:** The student-facing wellbeing experience is incomplete. Stress information does not yet safely reduce workload, pause pressure-based reminders or adjust recommendations after teacher review.

### 87. First Aid Training in Kindergartens

**Author shown in the DOCX:** Not listed in the summary  
**EEC completion:** **25% — Upcoming**

**What the paper means:** Young children can learn useful practical skills when the lesson is structured, age-appropriate and supported by adults.

**What EEC already has:**

- Teachers can upload first-aid or practical-skills learning material.
- Teachers can create lessons with an introduction, demonstration, guided practice, independent activity and recap.
- Quizzes, assignments and simple understanding checks can be added.
- The AI Tutor can answer questions using material approved by the teacher.

**What is still upcoming:** EEC has no dedicated first-aid course, approved child-safety content, practical demonstration check or certified safety process. This must remain teacher-led and should be reviewed by qualified first-aid experts.

## Management Conclusion

EEC is **not fully finished against these research papers**. The product already has a useful working foundation, especially for teacher-led AI, parent communication, assessments, learning support and student choice. The next stage is to connect these separate features into complete school workflows and measure whether they genuinely improve learning, wellbeing and teacher effectiveness.

The recommended business priority is:

1. Complete the teacher support and student intervention journey.
2. Add dedicated safeguarding and cyberbullying case handling.
3. Connect assessment, engagement, attendance and wellbeing safely for human review.
4. Give students clearer control over AI recommendations.
5. Add learning-impact, fairness and teacher-workload measurement.
6. Build off-screen activity and practical-learning support.

Until those steps are completed and tested with real schools, EEC should describe itself as **research-aligned and partly implemented**, not as fully research-proven.
